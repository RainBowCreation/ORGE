## 2 — Root cause (the one physics fact)
Water EOS descriptor `χ = (m_max − m_0)/(m_max − m_min) = (1000−1000)/(1000−125) = 0`. So an **under-filled**
water cell is on the EOS expansion branch `p = −K·χ·(…) = 0`. **Every under-filled water cell reads `p = 0`,
independent of how full it is.** A full cell (surface at 1.0·Δy) and a half-full cell (surface at 0.5·Δy)
are *indistinguishable* to the pressure field → §8.4 "uniform pressure ⇒ faces cancel ⇒ no flow."

The spec's pressure model (DEC-6: pressure-at-depth EMERGES from gravity + incompressible reflection over
ticks, `max==default`, no free-surface head) is **physically correct for OVERBURDEN** (weight stacked above,
captured by the persisted `p_dyn` / `divU` mechanism) but **structurally cannot represent a single cell's own
partial-fill free-surface head** — the surface drop lives *inside* the cell's geometry, which neighbours
cannot see (they only see `p=0`).

**SCENE 1 = SCENE 2 (same gap).** SCENE 2's column *can* start slumping (its base builds `p_dyn` from the 3
cells above), but the moment it spreads into a single-layer tail, every tail cell is under-filled `p=0` =
the SCENE-1 condition → leveling stalls. The "~1/9 columns then stops" is exactly this. The handoff's STEP-0
split (fix SCENE2, escalate SCENE1) misses this coupling.

## 4 — What this is NOT (so the amendment is honest)
The free-surface head is **neither** forbidden mechanism:
- **NOT an EOS compression band** — it fires ONLY for liquids at/below `m_rest`; it never widens `max_mass`
  (`max==default` stands) and never makes the cell over-compressible.
- **NOT a global "Σ mass above"** — it reads ONLY the cell's own stored `m` (like the EOS already does);
  fully local, GPU-ideal, computed per-cell in ENCRYPT; transmits nothing through load-bearing solids.
It is the LOCAL **complement** to overburden: overburden = head from above (already emergent via `p_dyn`),
free-surface = head from the cell's OWN fill.

## 5 — PROPOSED amendment (to validate via TDD, not ship blind)
Add to ENCRYPT's scalar pressure, for liquid cells (χ ≈ 0), a hydrostatic free-surface head:
```
φ        = clamp(m / m_rest, 0, 1)              # fill fraction (own state only)
p_surf   = ½ · ρ_rest · g · φ · Δy             # cell-avg head of the partial fluid column
                                                # ρ_rest = m_rest / V  (the fluid is at rest density)
```
- Applies to FULL cells too (`φ=1 ⇒ p_surf = ½ρ_rest gΔy`), so **full > half ⇒ flow goes the right way**.
- For submerged full cells at equal depth `p_surf` is uniform ⇒ adds a constant offset ⇒ **§J.5 gradient
  (what drives flow) is unchanged** ⇒ hydrostatic balance preserved (verify in test).
- SCENE 1: full `p_surf=5000`, half `p_surf=2500` → gradient 2500 → same-species flux (already allowed)
  levels to `750/750` (both `p_surf=3750`). ✓
- SCENE 2: gives the spreading tail a driver to finish leveling **after** the cross-species mover (§6) gets
  water onto the floor.

⚠ **Subtle:** a free surface in a cell-averaged incompressible scheme is the classic VOF hard problem. The
formula above is a shallow-water-style heuristic that gives the right horizontal driver; it MUST be validated
to not destabilize the vertical free surface (water must not fountain up into air, nor collapse). If it can't
satisfy all invariants (§7), STOP and escalate rather than ship.

## 6 — SCENE 2's second piece (horizontal cross-species displacement, the T5-deferred mover)
For water to reach the floor air at all, the `crossOccluded` no-flux must be lifted for the water→compressible
case. Air is compressible (χ_air≈1) ⇒ §C.5 says the flux *passes through* (air absorbs) and §8.5 says the
lowest-`p` reachable cell is consumed. This is conservation-CRITICAL (one-species-per-cell ⇒ the displaced
air must go somewhere, conserved). Options to design under TDD: continuous flux into the compressible air with
the air pushed to the lowest-`p` reachable neighbour (a displacement/push path), respecting `f ≥ min` (§8.7).
**If it cannot be made exact-conservative + bounded overnight, DEFER it + escalate — do not ship a leak.**
